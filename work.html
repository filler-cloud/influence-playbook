Work

Returning to BCG and building a career in sports investing will demand influence at multiple levels — clients, internal leadership, partners, and co-investors. Influence here isn't about commanding or convincing; it's about framing opportunities, building coalitions, celebrating early wins, and letting others feel ownership of the outcome.

Influence Moves to Use:

  • Frame Choices, Not Ultimatums: Give clients and partners 2-3 strategic paths forward instead of pushing one agenda. Let them feel agency in the decision-making process.
  • Build Coalitions Before You Enter the Room: Major pitches and big moments are won quietly in advance — through hallway conversations, quiet buy-in, and pre-alignment with key stakeholders.
  • Anchor Big Visions to Early Small Wins: Start by delivering tangible mini-successes (quick projects, proof points) that create momentum for larger strategies.
  • Use Foot-in-the-Door Influence: Start with manageable asks (e.g., a pilot project) to build confidence and credibility before proposing major investments or strategic overhauls.
  • Lead with Warmth Before Showing Expertise: Clients and colleagues trust you before they trust your ideas. Emotional resonance unlocks cognitive influence later.

Mini Challenge:

Before your next client or internal meeting, have 1-2 side conversations beforehand to quietly build early support for your idea.

Questions to Ask Myself:

  • Am I making it emotionally easy for others to buy in?
  • Have I done the behind-the-scenes work before the big meeting?
  • Am I celebrating small, visible wins early enough?

carry with you the lessons you've learned.
